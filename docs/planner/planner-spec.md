## 3. Date and rule evaluation

### 3.1 Dates and ownership of the field

Let dates be Gregorian day ordinals, so adding one moves to the next calendar day. For season i, the known facts must satisfy:

$$s_i \le h_i < e_i \le r_i,\qquad p_i \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}.$$

- The crop period is `[sᵢ,eᵢ)`. The harvest/final cut date `hᵢ` is included. Set `e=h+1` only when the profile or explicitly declared calendar defines the harvest/cut as the last crop day. A berseem cut followed by regrowth or seed retention is not the final crop endpoint.
- Same-field occupation is `[sᵢ,rᵢ)`, including any required removal or access delay. `rᵢ` is the first day available for preparation. `[rᵢ,rᵢ+pᵢ)` reserves exactly `pᵢ` full days. With known, applicable dates and duration the next sowing passes at equality:

$$s_{i+1} \ge r_i+p_i \quad (i=1,2,3).$$

- Require each later sowing to be on or after every earlier slot’s known release, including across a middle slot with unknown dates. With resolved transitions the three adjacent checks imply the rest. If a later slot sows before an earlier slot starts, report a slot-order violation rather than claiming that the occupations overlap. `p₄` has no successor in this horizon. The final season still needs a supported crop end and release for a complete schedule. Any known declared `hᵢ+1`, `eᵢ` or protected inclusive through-date plus one bounds `rᵢ` from below. Add a known applicable minimum preparation duration for the immediate successor. If the next sowing falls before a proven bound, record failure even with an unknown exact release. A lower bound that is met cannot prove a pass. Preparation never fills in an unknown release.
- A known conflict in the confirmed calendar is grounds for rejection of that calendar. A check using illustrative inputs explains its result as conditional on those inputs and retains the independent scientific review gates. Unknown removal time, preparation, or field workability stays unknown. A zero-day turnaround is legal only when explicitly supported for the transition.

### 3.2 Commitments, sowing windows and applicability

- Crop and purpose locks use equality to the confirmed protected value. An exact `harvest_or_cut_date` lock requires `h=D` and that slot’s crop present on D, with `sᵢ ≤ D < eᵢ`. `occupation_through(D)` requires `sᵢ ≤ D < rᵢ`. An exact protected cut or inclusive occupation lock constrains later occupation even if the proposed endpoint is missing. Emit a proven failure when known facts contradict a lock. Emit unknown when its retention cannot be established. List a lock in `preserved_commitment_ids` when its own season retains the protected value, even if the independent same-field rule proves another slot conflicts with it. This describes retention in the plan. Keep the original obligations in every investigation. Generation cannot rewrite them or fill unknown harvest dates from the obligation alone.
- Check `sᵢ` against a profile's inclusive interval `[a,b]` (equivalent to `[a,b+1)`). Resolve boundaries for the specific slot and year from the pinned source-backed profile. Keep discrete supported sowings separate from a continuous window. Qualitative endpoints such as “mid-September” remain advisory text until exact boundaries are supported. Mark a preference outside its band as an advisory failure. An unsupported date with no matching approved basis for eligibility yields a separate required planning unknown. Only an applicable demonstrated exclusion, documented by its source or a confirmed field restriction, can fail a hard eligibility rule.
- Match applicability before testing a numeric threshold or emitting positive support. Known mismatch makes that source's rule `not_applicable` with a reason. Missing facts leave it `unknown`. If no remaining applicable evidence covers a required topic, emit its own required planning unknown. Where sources conflict, keep both with their scopes. Use an explicitly resolved, versioned precedence when justified. Otherwise leave the required conclusion open. Never default a missing soil class, EC extraction, irrigation availability, stage or cultivar from a weather point or trial plot.
- A source-supported, versioned mapping may be a scoped exact schedule or an approved date-dependent function. It returns a justified `h,e,r` or explicit unknowns and cites its inputs, management and validity range. A verified operational release/preparation plan may supply corresponding fields under documented applicability. A reported maturity range, advisory window or observed elapsed interval alone supplies no exact endpoints. If bounds are used, use them only to prove the inequality in the appropriate direction and retain unknown fields. For v1 feasibility, require a supported concrete calendar as well.
- A matched source can support a field restriction if its required measurement/method matches, or if a confirmed operational constraint explicitly contradicts an applicable requirement, such as no permitted irrigation during a required delivery period. A qualitative suitability recommendation can require review. It supplies no numeric cutoff. Unknown feed quantity is advisory unless a confirmed hard quantity requirement needs a separate verified check. Optional demand or stage-only information needed for replay does not determine planning status. Agronomic maturity and harvest evidence needed for scheduling remain required.

$$\operatorname{status}(P)=
\begin{cases}
\texttt{rejected} & \text{if an applicable hard check fails},\\
\texttt{needs\_review} & \text{if any required planning check is unresolved},\\
\texttt{feasible} & \text{otherwise, after all required planning checks are covered}.
\end{cases}$$

Here `not_applicable` requires a known reason. It only discharges a planning topic if that topic is genuinely outside scope, such as preparation after the final slot. It cannot substitute for a missing applicable source. Feasible means supported by the pinned rules for the stated field and commitments. It is not a prediction of yield, biomass or farm outcome.

## 4. Deterministic candidate search

### 4.1 Candidate construction and four-season propagation

For each slot, gather the baseline choice and the union of canonical allowed values for each permitted field (`crop_id`, `profile_id`, `sowing_date`). Retain the originating choice IDs. The choices represent independently confirmed alternatives. Do not infer permission to combine edits where the farmer's intent was conditional or mutually exclusive. Use an independently confirmed scope the v1 types can express in that case. Keep `purpose`, site, area, locks and all unpermitted decisions fixed.

Resolve crop/profile consistency jointly. A direct same-crop profile change must be explicitly permitted. For a crop substitution, bind an explicitly permitted compatible profile, or a uniquely identified source-supported profile for that authorized substitution and unchanged purpose. Use the unchanged sowing only if supported for the new profile or use a permitted sowing choice. If no supported profile/date can be bound, record the choice as uninstantiated. Do not invent a calendar for a crop substitution. A permitted same-profile timing change with a real date can be materialized for investigation even when its eligibility or endpoints are open. Keep `kind: timing_variant` for sowing-only edits. For profile/crop identity edits use `crop_change`, or `combined` when a sowing also changes. A profile-only explanation must explicitly name the cultivar/profile change and the unchanged crop.

For a changed sowing, crop, purpose or profile, recompute all dependent dates and management assumptions. Invalidate old `h,e,r` and stage assignments unless the pinned mapping or a documented independent fact still applies. Set each unsupported endpoint to a null `Fact` with `unknown_reason: endpoint_mapping_missing`, `origin: unknown`, `evidence_status: open` and empty source refs when no applicable source exists. Candidate-generated changed facts are proposals awaiting confirmation, so use `confirmation_status: unconfirmed` for those facts, including computed unknowns. On confirmation the user may acknowledge nulls as `confirmed_unknown` without upgrading evidence. Preserve previously confirmed facts where independent. Recheck `pᵢ` for applicability to the new transition. Carry an independently confirmed transition policy only when its scope still matches. Unknown preparation remains null. Do not move a later season automatically. Evaluate every slot, all three adjacency edges, and earlier known occupation bounds against all later sowings. Changed conditions propagate through the checks and invalidated derived facts. Any downstream edit is a new independently permitted combination.

### 4.2 Enumeration, ranking and bounds

Use a simple Cartesian enumeration with a deterministic budget. Normalize canonical IDs, dates and aliases through the pinned catalogue. Sort values with baseline first, then canonical ID/date order. Enumerate tuples in a fixed order by number of directly edited slots (1–4), then ordered combinations of season orders, then product of each edited slot's nonbaseline field combinations. This prioritizes small repairs under the budget without using crop science to prune. An unchanged tuple is handled once as the current plan.

$$N_{raw} \le \prod_{i=1}^{4}(1+c_i)(1+f_i)(1+d_i)-1$$

Here `cᵢ`, `fᵢ` and `dᵢ` count distinct additional permitted crop IDs, profile IDs and dates for a slot. There are at most four crop IDs overall. Propose **256 raw alternative tuples** as the first version's configurable work budget, chosen to cover the full product of four choices per slot (255 alternatives) and bound explainable output size. This is an engineering budget to benchmark. Count malformed combinations, duplicates and uninstantiated tuples against that budget. Stop before tuple 257, even if only two outputs are sought. Use input-size limits from the service to bound normalization, and lazy products to bound the number of built tuples. Keep generated results within response limits at the API boundary. There is no wall-clock-dependent tie break. If a request times out, surface service failure rather than report a complete search.

Exact duplicate key is the ordered canonical tuple per season of `(season_id, crop_id, purpose, profile_id, s, h, e, r, p)`, with a canonical null. Deduplicate only within the same fixed inputs/rules and same decision/evidence semantics. When two paths describe the same normalized plan and evidence, merge choice provenance and evaluate once. If provenance changes a rule outcome, keep its distinct evidence context for evaluation. Include that context in the internal key. Stable candidate IDs derive from the base dependency reference and canonical key, without locale, template wording or enumeration position. Server response IDs and timestamps can differ across requests. Drop exact baseline equivalents.

Only feasible results enter ranking. The confirmed `understand_demand` and `understand_sequence` priorities ask for explanations and comparative evidence. They specify no direction or exchange rate. Keep them in the confirmed order in presentation. When `minimize_changes` is present, order lexicographically by:

1. Count of directly edited seasons.
2. Count of directly changed crop/profile choices and sowing dates, counting a crop substitution and its dependent profile rebinding once. Exclude derived harvest/release adjustments.
3. Count of changed dates with unknown baseline displacement, then the sum of known absolute day shifts.

Then break ties by the ordered canonical crop/profile/date key. If `minimize_changes` is absent, use the stable key alone. These are counts and elapsed days, with no numeric weights or assumed priority for crop preservation. Explain the actual changes and consequences in `tradeoffs`. Never rank eligibility by NASA data, a forecast or a soil score.

Offer the first feasible candidate and then the first meaningfully distinct feasible candidate in this order, if any. The initial display grouping key is the crop/purpose sequence, directly edited slot IDs and any applicable, source-backed management or recommended-window decision that changes the farmer's action. A different cultivar counts for grouping when it has a sourced material management/calendar distinction. Multiple sow dates within the same decision context share one display group. Do not infer a different trade-off from a bare profile alias, minor date offset or different source ID. Exact duplicates are removed before evaluation. Display grouping only affects the two feasible alternatives. Review and rejected lists remain separate and use stable ordering.

`search.evaluated_count` counts distinct materialized alternatives actually evaluated across all statuses, excluding P0. `scope_choice_ids` lists the confirmed scope. `uninstantiated_choice_ids` reports IDs with at least one evidence-blocked allowed value for which no concrete plan could be built in the visited scope. A proven incompatible tuple is a domain exclusion. A choice unavailable due to evidence is uninstantiated. Set `search.status: incomplete` for a budget stop, deferred enumeration, or an uninstantiated evidence branch. Use a stable `limit_reason` in precedence order `candidate_budget`, `enumeration_deferred`, `uninstantiated_choices`, otherwise null. Complete means exhausted within the confirmed discrete scope. Report “zero feasible alternatives in the searched scope” when empty.

### 4.3 Pseudocode

```text
promise_test(input_ref, catalogue_version, rules_version):
    snapshot, catalogue, rules = load_and_verify_pinned_inputs(...)
    current = evaluate_all(snapshot.seasons, snapshot, catalogue, rules)
    scope = canonical_confirmed_choices(snapshot.exploration, catalogue)
    results = empty map
    uninstantiated = empty set
    for tuple in ordered_raw_alternatives(scope, snapshot.seasons), up to budget:
        outcome = materialize(tuple, snapshot, catalogue)
        if outcome is evidence_blocked:
            record blocked choice values and continue
        if outcome is proven domain exclusion:
            continue
        plan = recompute_or_invalidate_dependent_facts(outcome.plan, snapshot)
        key = canonical_key(plan, applicable_evidence_context)
        if plan equals baseline or key already in results:
            merge provenance if applicable and continue
        checks = evaluate_all(plan, snapshot, catalogue, rules)
        results[key] = candidate_with_changes(plan, checks)
    resolve uninstantiated choice IDs from visited blocked/instantiated values
    feasible = stable_rank(results with status feasible, snapshot.priority_order)
    return PlanningResult(current, first_two_distinct_display_groups(feasible),
                          stable_review_and_rejected_lists(results),
                          search_metadata(scope, results, uninstantiated, budget))
```

`evaluate_all` checks scope/identity, each lock, each per-slot planning topic, then field occupancy and preparation with interval and evidence predicates. It also returns optional explanation and demand readiness checks. It performs no network requests. For `n=4`, `Q` total per-slot/transition rule work plus commitment and requirement checks, and at most `B=256` attempted alternatives, evaluation and ranking cost `O((B+1)(n²+Q) + B log B)` after normalizing accepted input. Storage for the result is `O(B(n+Q))` with lazy enumeration. Four slots imply three adjacent edges and at most six earlier/later occupancy pairs per plan. Rule functions must run over bounded profile/field facts, without weather-dependent loops in eligibility.

## 6. Season Replay interface

1. **Resolve inputs and versions.** Yahya’s later `POST /replays` resolves immutable `InputRef`, baseline `PlanRef`, candidates, requested block IDs and pinned data, catalogue/rules, mapping, coverage and calculation versions. The primary comparison requires feasible plans. A rejected/review baseline may have explicitly hypothetical diagnostics if all required intervals and declared assumptions support the metric. An explicitly hypothetical paired comparison may include that reference only when the required metrics are supported on the same blocks and each status is shown. Keep such results separate from the feasible comparison and never infer achievable savings from them. A missing endpoint suppresses dependent metrics. Never promote a calendar through replay.
2. **Map one common horizon.** Fix consecutive calendar-year blocks for the compared set. The common horizon spans the calendar years of the four season slots and all required calendar dates, including operational release/preparation, under the declared mapping. Pin a common source anchor year Y, the first slot’s start year, and the number L of consecutive years through the last required date. Use the half-open weather block `[Jan 1 of y, Jan 1 of y+L)`, with `shared_blocks.end_date` exclusive under this mapping version. An exclusive endpoint exactly at the block boundary is representable. For each eligible historical anchor y, map explicit `(Y+k,m,d)` to `(y+k,m,d)`. Apply to sowings and protected explicit dates. Recompute supported crop/harvest/release endpoints via the profile for the mapped date and carry only applicable preparation and management. Rerun eligibility and locks across all four mapped seasons. Include intervening leap days by elapsed calendar arithmetic. If an explicit 29 February cannot be represented, a required date exceeds the fixed horizon, or any compared feasible plan fails or needs review after mapping, exclude that anchor for all paired plans with a reason. Never slide an explicit commitment. Use one shared set of eligible block IDs and dates. For a hypothetical pair apply the same common mapping and coverage. Rerun checks and retain the diagnostic baseline’s mapped conflicts. Those conflicts alone do not invalidate weather exposure over its complete declared intervals.
3. **Read validated regional weather.** Use the prepared **2001–2025** Sakha POWER daily point release when packaged under `DATA_SPEC.md`, **31.10° N, 30.95° E**, returned grid elevation **4.96 m**, AG, **LST**. LST date keys are joined directly to mapped civil date labels without DST or hour shifts. Require unique site/date/LST/release keys and finite, valid `et0_mm_day` with method ID, QC, provenance and units. ET0 uses `tmax_c`, `tmin_c`, `tdew_c` (°C), `wind2_m_s` (m/s at 2 m), `solar_mj_m2_day` (MJ/m²/day) and `pressure_kpa` (kPa), along with pinned source geometry and day-of-year. Retain `rh_percent` and `rain_mm_day` (mm/day) for QC/context. Missing RH/rain alone does not invalidate valid ET0. Treat the separately audited 2026 quick-look data as a different release context. Preserve original data vintage and the documented regional representativeness limitation. [NASA POWER Daily API](https://power.larc.nasa.gov/docs/services/api/temporal/daily/).
4. **Use a reproducible coverage policy.** Proposed initial policy requires a complete common block of valid daily ET0 and Tmax for every main comparison. Reject duplicate or incompatible releases/units, retain quality flags, and exclude missing/invalid required days for all plans. Use optional metrics on that same block set. If an optional metric is not available on every included block for both plans, mark that paired summary unavailable and identify missing plan/block metrics. No imputation, no per-plan year selection, and no silent zero for missing rain/ET0/Kc. Return `unavailable` for no common eligible blocks. Use `partial` when the common core is available and dependent optional metrics are unavailable. Record exclusions, flags and coverage version.
5. **Compute exposure and paired differences.** For each plan, slot and block, use crop interval `[s,e)`. Sum audited **FAO56 ET0 with the ASCE daily cloudiness bound** in mm over that period and show its duration, crop-free gaps `[eᵢ,sᵢ₊₁)` where nonnegative, preparation/occupancy and retained grain/forage commitments. Crop-free is a calendar indicator. Compute mean daily Tmax and maximum daily Tmax over known crop intervals with complete data. With an explicitly declared descriptive °C threshold θ, count days where `Tmax≥θ` and label exposure only. Stage-specific exposure requires dated stage support. A crop-risk label requires an applicable sourced threshold with crop/cultivar, stage, temperature statistic, duration convention, scope and compatible data. No inferred yield or risk from a daily maximum alone.
6. **Keep the approved demand method.** Use the already-audited ET0 release and `SCIENCE.md` §3 when reproducing the calculation. No method change is proposed. Only compute standard-condition `ETc(t)=Kc(t)×ET0(t)` when an applicable approved stage/coefficient curve exists, sums to the crop period, and matches reference surface and management. Use the approved science curve convention when authorized, including daily midpoint evaluation. Sum over `[s,e)`. ET0 is reference atmospheric demand, ETc is conditional crop demand, and equivalent volume `10 × depth_mm × area_ha` is an ET volume in m³. Neither rainfall nor those metrics establish withdrawals, irrigation delivery, or savings. No exact marginal extra-cut water estimate from averaged berseem coefficients. [FAO56 crop coefficients and stages](https://www.fao.org/4/x0490e/x0490e0b.htm).
7. **Return evidence, not a winner.** For each metric M with common support report `dᵧ=Mᵧ(candidate)−Mᵧ(baseline)` inside each block, then count, median, min/max and positive/negative/tied counts. A reversal means at least one positive and one negative difference. Count repeated years transparently if anchor blocks overlap, without treating blocks as independent replicates. Return `ReplayResult` with shared blocks, exclusions, units, provenance, statuses and paired differences. Keep the calendar-only comparison available for the project’s usefulness check with the same plans and inputs.
8. **Link the Evidence Card.** Assemble the approved `EvidenceCard` with the exact input/planning/replay/forecast references, `plan_status`, `decision_state`, `currency`, commitments, edits, assumptions, outstanding checks, source references and versioned takeaway template. Drill down to mapped block IDs, exclusions, metric status and methods through the immutable replay reference. Arabic and English/audio use the same parameters. Material edits or dependency changes invalidate currentness and approval under the existing contracts. A missing replay/forecast does not prevent saving a review draft.
