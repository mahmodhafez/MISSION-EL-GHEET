# MISSION EL GHEET — agronomy validation audit

Checked 10–11 November 2026. Scope: public evidence for one Egyptian locality and the four proposed crops; no farmer records, expert review, field measurements, software implementation or crop recommendations were produced.

## 11 November source-access update

An original Ministry/ARC maize bulletin **1429/2023**, hosted by Misr Elzraea, is now verified. Printed page4 recommends **May for old lands**; its mid-May–mid-June guidance is for specified new-land areas, so do not apply that range indiscriminately to Sakha. Page5 describes suitable soil as fertile, well drained/aerated and non-saline. [Original bulletin](https://misrelzraea.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/%D9%86%D8%B4%D8%B1%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B0%D8%B1%D8%A9-%D9%86%D9%87%D8%A7%D8%A6%D9%89-copy_compressed.pdf).

The Central Agricultural Extension account also publishes older **2014** old-land guidance extending sowing to mid-June. [Original extension post](https://kenanaonline.com/users/Caaes/posts/598687). The two dates should retain their source/year/context. For a conservative first old-land research catalogue, use May as the supported preference interval; dates outside it are outside this catalogue's established support, not proof that cultivation is biologically impossible. A May date shift still requires a stated release/preparation interval and cultivar-duration assumptions. This closes the missing published maize-window lead, not every phenology or crop-repair gap below.

## Decision

**Retain Sakha, Kafr El-Sheikh, as a published research scenario. Agricultural feasibility is partially supported, not fully validated.** Among the sources examined, Sakha has the strongest combination of measured soil, named wheat/berseem cultivars, and an observed wheat–grain-maize succession. There is insufficient evidence here to certify every permitted date shift, every promised berseem cut, grain sorghum as a locally interchangeable option, or three feasible four-season plans.

No adviser is required to reproduce an honestly labelled research scenario. Absence of adviser access does not turn unknown agronomy into a valid rule: unsupported options must remain unavailable or explicitly exploratory. A source scenario is not a farmer's field assessment.

Status meanings below: **V** = verified statement in an opened source; **A** = proposed modelling assumption; **U** = unresolved. A verified trial observation is not automatically a current extension recommendation.

## One usable conflict, without inventing an agronomic deadline

**Proposed test fixture, not an observed farmer case:** a farmer protects a final berseem forage cut on **20 May**, while an existing same-field plan has maize sowing on **15 May**. The latter date has an observed Sakha example in Aiad's study; the protected cut date is a deliberately declared fixture input, not a published Sakha 4 recommendation.

The planner can prove these two same-field occupations overlap. It must preserve the cut, identify the conflicting maize entry and explain the smallest admissible change. It cannot conclude that maize must be abandoned: the cited trial did not establish 15 May as the last viable date. A later maize candidate requires its own locally supported sow-to-release case, plus preparation time. If that is absent, return **“No verified repair in the current crop catalogue”**, rather than manufacture an alternative or secretly remove forage.

The present sources support a historical wheat→maize baseline and local winter forage context, **not a fully validated four-season berseem→maize→wheat→sorghum sequence**. A calendar repeated into a second year is an assumption. A protected late cut also does not automatically cause a conflict two seasons later; show only consequences actually implied by the dates and constraints.

## Minimal defensible model contract

1. Each catalogue entry records locality, source/year, cultivar or crop type, purpose, observed dates or supported window, harvest definition, stage provenance, and evidence status. Keep **observed example**, **recommended window**, and **assumed scenario** separate.
2. Each field has one timeline. The end of crop occupation includes required harvest/removal/preparation time; an unknown value must not silently become zero. Farmer commitments are user inputs, not evidence that a yield or feed amount will be achieved.
3. The solver protects those commitments before changing unprotected crops/dates. It can guarantee consistency with encoded constraints; it cannot claim biological feasibility beyond their evidence. Missing rules produce an explicit unavailable result.
4. Replay comparable plans over the same historical weather years, area and horizon. Distinguish in-crop ETc from uncovered periods. A shorter crop or empty season does not automatically make a plan better; grain and forage are different outputs and their quantities are not predicted here.
5. Soil/sequence output stays descriptive: legume presence, cereal repetition, occupied/unoccupied dates, source-scenario soil restrictions. Do not assert restored fertility, nitrogen credits, disease reduction, equal feed, or measured root benefits for this field. A legume's presence alone does not quantify retained nitrogen after forage removal.
6. ET0 can be shown independently of crop-stage support. With insufficient stages, suppress crop-specific ETc ranking or present only explicitly assumed sensitivity scenarios; never call them locally validated. Near-term model output cannot create a planting window or crop action absent from the agronomic catalogue.

## Remaining gates before agricultural claims

- **Four-crop catalogue:** find primary Sakha/closely matched evidence for grain sorghum, current crop-type suitability, and release dates; otherwise narrow the supported crop set visibly.
- **A real multicut schedule:** obtain a published Sakha berseem schedule with cultivar, planting date and individual cut dates, or make the dates farmer-entered commitments without promising their biological performance.
- **Stages and repair ranges:** establish local stage partitions and at least one viable later-sowing maize case; validate date variants separately from crop substitutions. A grain-maturity observation is insufficient for every field-release date.
- **Scenario integrity:** use one traceable published soil profile, with measurement depth/method. Do not attach it to a user's location as if measured there. Keep saline trial conditions separate from stress-free ETc assumptions.
- **Source access:** ARC crop bulletins/catalogue pages and the full Sugita et al. (2017) crop-ET paper remained inaccessible during this audit. Indexed fragments were used as leads only. The promising [Sugita author manuscript](https://tsukuba.repo.nii.ac.jp/record/41521/files/HydrologicalSciencesJournal_20170314.pdf), DOI 10.1080/02626667.2017.1341631, may contain a stronger Sakha succession, but no rule above depends on an unverified fragment.

**Readiness conclusion:** enough public evidence to construct and review a carefully labelled Sakha historical scenario; insufficient evidence to claim the complete four-crop repair catalogue, exact forage preservation, or farmer-specific agronomic advice is validated. No population benefit, water saving, expert endorsement or field usability is established by this audit.
